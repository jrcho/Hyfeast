    C.3 Experimental Comparison of the Earij U2 RC Beam

    Experimental comparison. The U2 specimen tested by Alfano et al. (2012) is assessed using the data reported by Earij et al. (2017). The 2D plane-stress model uses perfectly bonded embedded reinforcement and tensile stress–strain inputs. Intermediate unloading/reloading is omitted; only the monotonic loading envelope is compared. Residual deflection and unloading stiffness are outside scope.

    Model and materials

    The beam spans 2,600 mm, has an overall length of 2,760 mm, width of 150 mm and depth of 300 mm. Loading points are 600 mm apart. The half-span CPS4 model connects surface support/loading nodes to reference nodes with free rotation.

    Concrete modulus is 26 GPa, converted compressive strength 15.512 MPa and tensile strength 1.8 MPa. Reinforcement uses 205 GPa modulus and 380 MPa yield stress with perfect plasticity. Hordijk tensile curves are converted to mesh-specific plastic-strain coordinates. Strain input does not automatically account for element length.

    Figure C.3.1 U2 Beam, Reinforcement and Medium Mesh

    Experiment, published analysis and current results

    Total experimental load is twice the absolute half-span loading-point reaction. Peak errors use the reported experimental peak of 60.5 kN; curve errors use the loading envelope extracted from Figure 13(b).

    Input Concrete elements Viscosity Peak total load Peak error Curve NRMSE
    u2-medium 696 \(10^{-5}\) 57.574 kN −4.84% 2.95%
    u2-medium-stable 696 \(10^{-4}\) 57.654 kN −4.70% 2.92%
    u2-fine-stable 2,784 \(10^{-4}\) 58.080 kN −4.00% 2.70%

    All three calculations complete the prescribed loading displacement. Published EX.STR curve NRMSE, calculated on the same basis, is 2.47%. The published model is 3D with cyclic loading; the current model is 2D with monotonic loading. Comparison is limited to their common loading path.

    Peak-load changes from mesh and viscosity variations are +0.74% and +0.14%, respectively. Early-response discrepancies remain despite small overall curve errors; response beyond the final compression input point includes extrapolation assumptions. These results do not establish local crack/crushing convergence or zero-viscosity behavior.

    Figure C.3.2 U2 Experiment, Published EX.STR and Current CDP Results

    Input conversion and material-point checks

    Hordijk tensile softening uses fracture energy 0.0353542 N/mm. Crack opening is divided by the medium/fine mesh lengths of 25/12.5 mm and converted to the current CDP plastic-strain coordinate.

    \[ \varepsilon_t^{ck}=\frac{w}{h},\qquad d_t=1-\frac{\sigma_t}{f_t},\qquad \kappa_t=\varepsilon_t^{ck}-\frac{d_t}{1-d_t}\frac{\sigma_t}{E}. \]

    Compression is likewise converted from the published inelastic strain and damage to plastic strain. Residual tensile strength is 1% and the damage cap is 0.99. Maximum stress differences between material-point responses and transformed curves are 0.0000471 MPa in tension and 0.000751 MPa in compression. Reaction-derived stress differences are 0.0000667 and 0.000733 MPa, respectively. A separate steel material-point calculation confirms a 380 MPa plateau after yield.

    Figure C.3.3 Transformed Tensile and Compressive Laws and Actual Material-Point Responses

    Compression strength/damage from published Figure 7 and experimental data from Figure 13(b) are preserved with axis calibrations. The reported experimental peak of 60.5 kN differs from the approximately 59.59 kN peak traced from the figure. The published 46.1 kN marks unloading initiation, not first cracking.

    Figure C.3.4 Digitized Source Curves and Axis Calibration

    Complete records of the three structural analyses

    All cases reach loading-point displacement −26.5 mm. Deflections below are measured at the concrete midspan bottom and differ from loading-point displacement. Times describe the executable and environment used for the original calculations.

    Table C.3.1 Peak, Final State and Execution Records

    Input Midspan deflection at peak (mm) Final midspan deflection (mm) Final total load (kN) Increments Analysis time
    u2-medium 12.110 29.953 56.586 488 477.278 s
    u2-medium-stable 12.438 29.936 56.676 410 298.712 s
    u2-fine-stable 13.255 28.470 57.120 626 2,321.580 s

    Comparing the current analyses with the experimental loading envelope over 0–27.5 mm at 0.1 mm spacing gives RMSEs of 1.785, 1.764 and 1.632 kN. NRMSE divides RMSE by the reported 60.5 kN peak, giving 2.950, 2.916 and 2.697%. The earlier table that also includes EX.STR uses the common interval 0–27.4 mm, resulting in slightly different values.

    Figure C.3.5 All Three Completed U2 Analyses: Full and Initial Loading Responses

    Table C.3.2 Mesh and Viscosity Sensitivity over 0–27.5 mm

    Global response changes are small between the medium/fine meshes at viscosity \(10^{-4}\), but maximum local compression plastic strains differ: 0.00962 versus 0.05589. Fine-mesh \(10^{-5}\) and medium-mesh \(10^{-6}\) runs were interrupted because of long computation times and are excluded from completed results.

    Steel yield, damage fields and compression-table limits

    Across all frames, the maximum sum of loading/support reactions normalized by peak loading-point reaction is below 0.003%. Support vertical displacements and plane-stress conditions were checked; bottom steel stresses do not exceed 380 MPa.

    Table C.3.5 First Local Steel Yield and Compression-Table Extrapolation

    Input First local steel yield: deflection / total load (mm / kN) First compression-table exceedance (mm) NRMSE before exceedance Maximum compression plastic strain
    u2-medium 7.334 / 56.696 14.437 2.478% 0.00984
    u2-medium-stable 7.312 / 56.778 14.753 2.556% 0.00962
    u2-fine-stable 7.125 / 56.266 9.752 3.251% 0.05589

    The compression input ends at plastic strain approximately 0.002965, beyond which strength and damage remain constant. Some fine-mesh integration points exceed this limit before peak load, so its peak also includes this extension assumption. Pre-exceedance NRMSEs cover different interval lengths. First local exceedance does not establish crushing of the whole beam.

    Figure C.3.7 Tensile and Compressive Damage at the Medium-Mesh Peak Load

    The damage plot shows maximum integration-point values per element at the u2-medium peak. It is not a quantitative comparison with experimental crack widths or maps. Conclusions concern perfect bond, 2D geometry and monotonic loading; they do not extend to residual deflection, unloading stiffness or local crushing convergence.

    Complete numeric data

    • Completed-analysis summary, all-frame equilibrium/material checks, pointwise experimental errors
    • Mesh/viscosity sensitivity, errors on the published-analysis comparison basis, pointwise published/current comparisons
    • Material-point checks

    Full time, midspan deflection, total load, steel stress, compression strain and damage histories are in u2-medium-curve.csv, u2-medium-stable-curve.csv and u2-fine-stable-curve.csv in the case folder. Each run's inputs, HDB, logs and executable hashes remain under results. The first material-point execution's HDB/logs are separately preserved under original-results/material-point; the first and current CSVs are identical.

    Inputs and execution

    Examples/C-RCBeam-EarijU2/ preserves experimental/published curve coordinates, inputs, postprocessing and full results.

    Examples\C-RCBeam-EarijU2\run.bat u2-medium
Examples\C-RCBeam-EarijU2\run.bat u2-medium-stable
Examples\C-RCBeam-EarijU2\run.bat u2-fine-stable

python Examples\C-RCBeam-EarijU2\compare-reference.py

    Runs use repository-local tmp and preserve inputs/outputs in results. Source PDFs remain in the Research/CDP references folder. Postprocessing uses the HDB reader in the companion C-ConcreteBeam-Fracture case.
