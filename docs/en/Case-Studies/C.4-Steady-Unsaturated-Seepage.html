    C.4 Convergence and Efficiency of Steady Unsaturated Seepage Analysis

    Application. This case compares tangent treatment, step-size control and increment strategies for reaching a steady unsaturated seepage solution. It follows the final manuscript by Jeoung-Rae Cho, Dongchan Kim and Du Han Lee, Convergence and Computational Efficiency of Nonlinear Solution Strategies for Steady Unsaturated Seepage Analysis, and the merged results finalized on 2026-09-14. It is not an experimental comparison. The full-matrix figures below are historical paper results obtained with the executables and hardware used for that study.

    Model and Materials

    A 100 m square is modeled based on the problem of Paniconi and Putti (1994). The left boundary has a prescribed total head of 100 m, the top and bottom have no flow, and the right boundary is a potential seepage face (PSF). The rightmost 10 m uses the same water-retention curve as the main region and C times its saturated hydraulic conductivity.

    Figure C.4.1 Geometry and Boundary Conditions of the Steady Seepage Benchmark

    Item Conditions
    Element SC2D4, thickness 1 m
    Mesh factor M 1: \(10\times10\) elements; 5: \(50\times50\) elements
    Saturated-conductivity ratio C 1, 1000
    Main-region saturated conductivity \(10^{-6}\) m/s
    Unit weight of water 9810 N/m³
    Residual and saturated water contents 0.05, 0.40
    VGM \(\alpha\) 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10 m⁻¹
    VGM n 1.3, 1.5, 1.8, 2.2, 2.8

    The input parameter <aVG> is the head-based \(\alpha\). It is supplied as <aVG>/9810 to the pressure-based material model. AirEntry=Relative,0.05 applies when \(n\le2\); the original VGM relationship is used for \(n>2\). Geometry and boundary conditions remain the same when M and C change. A storage coefficient is specified in the material, but no storage term enters the steady-state equilibrium equation.

    Comparison Conditions

    Two meshes, two conductivity ratios and 25 VGM combinations give 100 model conditions. Nine tangent–step-control combinations and four increment strategies are applied to each condition, totaling 3,600 runs.

    Tangent treatment Step-size control
    Picard None, SchedRelax, RelaxBack
    Adaptive HNewton None, SchedRelax, RelaxBack
    Newton None, RelaxBack, ArmijoBack

    Adaptive HNewton is entered as HNewton,0,0.2,0.3,0.1,1000. The parameters are 0.01,0.65,10 for SchedRelax and 35,1E-9,1,0.5 for RelaxBack. Newton with ArmijoBack used the defaults at the time of the study.

    Strategy Steady-state Step dataline
    One: single-increment direct analysis Auto,1,1,1,1,1000,Path=Load,OutFreq=0
    Auto-Load: load continuation Auto,0.1,1,0.01,0.1,1000,Path=Load,OutFreq=0
    Auto-PTC: pseudo-transient continuation Auto,0.1,1,0.01,0.1,1000,Path=PTC,OutFreq=0
    Auto-Material: material continuation Auto,0.1,1,0.01,0.1,1000,Path=Material,OutFreq=0

    The steady-state progress value is not physical time. All strategies solve the same steady-state problem at the final progress value of 1. One fixes the minimum and maximum increments at 1, preventing cutback to a smaller increment.

    The paper matrix used Release executables with PARDISO, four parallel processes and four threads per process. The wall-time limit was 180 s per case, with at most 1,000 equilibrium iterations per increment. The pressure-correction tolerance was fixed at 10 Pa, and convergence-trend checks were deferred for the first 100 iterations. Flow balance was accepted when

    \[ e_Q=\frac{|Q_{out}-Q_{in}|}{\max(Q_{in},Q_{out})}\le10^{-4}. \]

    For a PSF 2-cycle, nodal inflow and positive-pressure violations are checked in addition to global flow balance. Ten affected cases were rerun on 2026-09-14 and incorporated into the final table.

    Paper Reference Results

    Of 3,600 runs, 1,029 succeeded, 2,456 failed and 115 timed out. Each tangent–step-control combination contains the same 400 attempts.

    Tangent treatment None SchedRelax RelaxBack ArmijoBack
    Picard 7 340 60 —
    Adaptive HNewton 7 288 104 —
    Newton 42 — 104 77

    Figure C.4.2 Converged Cases for the Nine Tangent and Step-Control Combinations

    Timing is compared only for conditions where both Picard+SchedRelax and the other combination converged. Adaptive HNewton+SchedRelax has a cumulative time ratio of 1.145 over 277 common cases; Newton+RelaxBack has a ratio of 11.556 over 103 common cases. Each comparison uses a different common-success set. These are not ratios of total run times including failures and timeouts.

    Figure C.4.3 Cumulative Wall-Time Ratios on the Common-Success Sets

    With Picard+SchedRelax, the success counts for the increment strategies are:

    One Auto-Load Auto-PTC Auto-Material
    74/100 90/100 77/100 99/100

    Figure C.4.4 Coverage of the Four Increment Strategies with Picard and Scheduled Relaxation

    PSF active sets matched in the 448 mutually converged strategy pairs using Picard+SchedRelax. Detailed pressure-field and through-flow comparisons are preserved in Result/results-endpoint-pair-summary.csv and Result/results-endpoint-pair-outliers.csv. These files also include other tangent treatments; select rows with TanReg=0 and StepCtrl=SchedRelax for the paper comparison. Small differences should be distinguished from evidence of different steady solutions.

    Representative Final Pressure Fields

    These are the four \((M,C)\) combinations with <aVG>=2, <n>=1.8, Auto-Material and Picard+SchedRelax. The black contour is zero pressure head; dots on the right mark pressure-pinned PSF nodes. The plots are drawn directly from final nodal values obtained with the pressure-based material model.

    Figure C.4.5 Representative Pressure-Head Fields and Active Seepage-Face Nodes

    For this problem family, Picard+SchedRelax was an effective default comparison, and Material continuation helped difficult conditions converge. This conclusion applies to the tested material, mesh and boundary conditions.

    Input Files and Execution

    Inputs are in Examples/C-Steady-Unsaturated-Seepage/ in the distribution folder. Copy them to a separate working folder before running hfAnalyzer. Configure PATH as described in Running hfAnalyzer.

    Strategy Picard Adaptive HNewton Newton
    One one-picard.inp one-hnewton.inp one-newton.inp
    Auto-Load load-picard.inp load-hnewton.inp load-newton.inp
    Auto-PTC ptc-picard.inp ptc-hnewton.inp ptc-newton.inp
    Auto-Material material-picard.inp material-hnewton.inp material-newton.inp

    All 12 inputs run independently. Their default condition is <M>=1, <C>=1, <aVG>=0.5, <n>=1.5. Picard and HNewton default to SchedRelax; Newton defaults to RelaxBack. Use parameter overrides for the representative pressure-field conditions.

    hfAnalyzer material-picard.inp
hfAnalyzer material-picard.inp -p "<M>=5,<C>=1000,<aVG>=2,<n>=1.8" -o representative-M5-C1000
hfAnalyzer load-newton.inp -p "<StepCtrl>={ArmijoBack}"

    Use a separate working folder for each material or solver run. *Print writes a nodal CSV named for the input into the working folder; rerunning the same input there overwrites that CSV. -o changes names of HDB and related results, but not the name explicitly supplied to *Print, File=....

    Preserved Results

    The final manuscript used as the comparison reference is preserved as coseik-steady-seepage-final.docx at the top level of the example folder.

    Result/seepage-final-results.xlsx contains overall summaries, the 3,600-case table, strategy-pair comparisons and nodal results for the four representative conditions. Result/case-results.csv preserves the original merged table unchanged; metadata.json records run provenance and hashes. The 144 SVG files under Result/grids/ show success, failure and timeout for each 25-material group.

    The example retains no hdb/rst/chk/log or full raw pressure-field files. The field_file and output columns identify historical artifacts and are not current file links. Representative pressure plots can be rebuilt from representative-fields.csv; recalculating all strategy-pair pressure comparisons requires rerunning the analyses. Compare current executable results and timings separately from the preserved paper results.
